### Topic: AI Technology

**California Gives Every State Agency Half-Price Claude**

- Governor **Gavin Newsom** announced on June 29 a first-of-its-kind deal letting every California state agency — and any city or county that opts in — access **Anthropic's Claude** at a **50% discount**, plus free staff training and technical help from Anthropic.
- Claude becomes the first AI tool offered through California's new **Statewide IT Shared Services (SITeS)** portal, a central marketplace for state agencies to buy AI tools at transparent, pre-negotiated prices.
- Some agencies were already piloting Claude before the deal: the **DMV** for customer service work, and the **Department of Health Care Services** for internal workflows.
- Newsom framed it around augmenting, not replacing, government workers: "AI should not replace the human work of government; it should help our workers move faster... and deliver better results for Californians."

**Why it matters:** This is the largest state-government AI rollout in the US so far — a real-world test of whether AI can make public services faster without cutting the humans out of the loop.

*Source: Office of Gov. Gavin Newsom / TechCrunch / CBS Sacramento, June 29, 2026*

### Topic: Environment

**Oceans Hit Record Heat as El Niño Builds**

- Europe's **Copernicus Climate Change Service** confirmed that global ocean surface temperatures hit a **record high in June 2026** — two independent Copernicus services measured it separately and got the same result.
- Scientists warn this record heat, combined with a developing **El Niño** (see the June 20 entry), puts the climate system into "uncharted territory," raising the odds of even more temperature records falling over the next few months.
- On the ground, the effect is already visible: more than **46 million people across the US** were under extreme heat alerts in early July, part of what climate scientists call one of the fastest-growing and deadliest weather threats.
- Forecasters now expect El Niño to strengthen rapidly through the July–September window, with model averages pointing toward a **strong event** (around +2.0°C above normal in the tropical Pacific).

**Why it matters:** Warmer oceans store and release more heat energy, fueling stronger heatwaves, storms, and coral bleaching — record-warm seas are an early warning sign for a hotter back half of 2026.

*Source: Copernicus Climate Change Service / WMO / NBC News, late June–July 2026*

### Topic: Economics

**China's Investment Slump Is the Worst Since 2020**

- China's **fixed-asset investment** (spending by companies and government on things like factories, property, and infrastructure) fell **4.1% year-on-year** in the January–May 2026 period — far worse than the 2% decline economists expected, and the steepest drop since **May 2020**.
- **Property investment** is the biggest drag, down **16.2%**, though infrastructure and manufacturing investment are still growing, just more slowly than earlier in the year.
- Retail sales fell **0.6%** in May from a year earlier — China's **first drop in retail spending since the end of Covid lockdowns in late 2022** — with car purchases down a sharp 16%.
- Together, weak spending by both businesses and consumers is piling pressure on Beijing to roll out a bigger stimulus package to prop up growth.

**Why it matters:** China is the world's second-largest economy, so a deepening slowdown there can ripple through global trade, commodity prices, and any country that sells into or relies on Chinese demand.

*Source: Bloomberg / CNBC / Reuters, June 16, 2026*

### Topic: AI Technology

**Tenstorrent CEO Denies Qualcomm's Billion-Dollar Bid**

- In mid-June, Reuters and several outlets reported that **Qualcomm** — best known for smartphone chips — was in talks to acquire **Tenstorrent**, an AI chip startup, for **$8–10 billion**.
- Tenstorrent designs chips using **RISC-V**, an open, royalty-free chip blueprint (unlike proprietary designs from Intel or Arm). Buying it would give Qualcomm its own AI chip technology to compete with **Nvidia** and **AMD**, who currently dominate the AI hardware market.
- The move fit a pattern: Qualcomm also confirmed a separate acquisition (of "Modular") the same week, together committing over **$14 billion** toward building AI hardware that doesn't rely on Nvidia.
- On **June 30**, Tenstorrent CEO **Jim Keller** publicly denied any acquisition talks with Qualcomm were taking place, directly contradicting the earlier reports and leaving the deal's status unclear.

**Why it matters:** Big tech companies are racing to reduce their dependence on Nvidia's AI chips — but this episode shows how unreliable early "deal talk" reporting can be, even when it moves stock prices.

*Source: Reuters / The Information / GuruFocus, June 15–30, 2026*

## 2026-06-21

### Topic: Economics

**Japan Hikes Rates to a 31-Year High**

- The **Bank of Japan** (BoJ) — Japan's central bank, which sets the cost of borrowing — raised its key interest rate by a quarter point to **1.00%** on **June 16, 2026**. That is the highest it has been since September 1995, a 31-year high.
- For decades Japan kept rates near or even below **zero** to fight the opposite problem: stubbornly *falling* prices and weak growth. So lifting rates this far signals a real shift — Japan now has lasting inflation to manage, like much of the world.
- The trigger is a **weak yen** (Japan's currency) and rising prices, worsened by the Iran war pushing up energy costs. Higher rates make the yen more attractive to hold and help cool inflation by making loans pricier.
- The board voted **7-1** in favour. Because markets had priced the move as near-certain (about 97% odds), the yen barely moved on the news.

**Why it matters:** Japan exiting its ultra-cheap-money era affects global markets, because investors have long borrowed cheaply in yen to invest elsewhere — so even small BoJ moves ripple worldwide.

*Source: CNBC / Al Jazeera, June 16, 2026*

## 2026-06-20

### Topic: Environment

**El Niño Is Back — Expect a Hotter Year**

- The UN's weather agency (the **WMO**) has confirmed that **El Niño has returned** and is likely to strengthen through the second half of 2026, with forecasters putting the odds of it lasting into November at **90% or higher**.
- *El Niño* is a natural climate pattern in which the central and eastern Pacific Ocean warms up more than usual. This shifts weather worldwide — typically pushing **global temperatures higher** and bringing more extreme weather, such as heatwaves in some regions and unusual droughts or heavy rain in others.
- Most forecast models expect at least a **moderate, possibly strong** event, though how intense it gets is still uncertain.
- The UN urged countries to strengthen **early-warning systems**, cautioning that temperatures will run above average "nearly everywhere." Combined with long-term warming, this makes it "virtually certain" 2026 will land among the **10 hottest years on record**.

**Why it matters:** El Niño sits on top of human-driven warming, so its return raises the risk of record heat, crop stress, and weather disasters — a reminder for vulnerable regions to prepare now.

*Source: WMO / UN News / NOAA, June 2026*

### Topic: Geopolitics

**Ethiopia's Ruling Party Heads for Landslide**

- Ethiopia, Africa's second-most populous country, held its **7th general election on June 1, 2026**, with more than **54 million registered voters** — one of the continent's largest democratic exercises this year.
- Early and verified vote counts show Prime Minister **Abiy Ahmed's Prosperity Party** far in front and on track for a sweeping win, echoing its landslide in 2021. Final nationwide results are being confirmed in mid-June.
- A general election is how voters choose members of parliament; the party that wins the most seats forms the government and its leader stays prime minister. A near-total win means little organised opposition to check the ruling party.
- The vote was **incomplete**: no voting took place in the entire **Tigray region** — still recovering from a recent war — and in parts of Amhara and Oromia, due to ongoing insecurity. Critics say this, plus a weak opposition, limits how representative the result is.

**Why it matters:** A landslide further cements Abiy's grip on a strategically important nation in the Horn of Africa, while unvoted conflict zones are a reminder that Ethiopia's internal divisions remain unresolved.

*Source: Bloomberg / Al Jazeera / Ethiopia's NEBE, June 2026*

## 2026-06-17

### Topic: Economics

**Fed Holds Rates as Inflation Lingers**

- The US Federal Reserve — the country's central bank, which sets the cost of borrowing — was widely expected to **keep its key interest rate unchanged** at its meeting concluding today, leaving the target range at **3.50%–3.75%**.
- The interest rate is the main lever the Fed uses to steer the economy: higher rates make loans pricier and cool spending, helping tame *inflation* (the rate at which prices rise); lower rates do the opposite.
- Rates have sat unchanged since December 2025 because inflation is still running **above the Fed's 2% goal**, hovering near three-year highs, partly due to high energy prices tied to the Middle East conflict.
- This is the **first meeting led by new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh**, sworn in last month. Markets are watching his projections for whether a rate cut is off the table — or even a future hike on it.

**Why it matters:** The Fed's rate shapes what you pay on mortgages, car loans, and credit cards, so a continued hold means borrowing stays expensive while the bank waits for inflation to ease.

*Source: CNBC / Kiplinger, June 17, 2026*

## 2026-06-16

### Topic: Environment

**AI's Hidden Power and Water Bill**

- A new United Nations University report measured the environmental footprint of AI. It found the world's **data centres** — the warehouses of computers that run AI services — used about **448 terawatt-hours of electricity in 2025**. If they were a country, that would make them the world's 11th-largest electricity user.
- A *terawatt-hour* is a billion kilowatt-hours; a typical home uses a few thousand kilowatt-hours a year. So this is an enormous amount of power, and AI accounts for about a fifth of it.
- Demand is climbing fast. The report projects data centres could reach **945 terawatt-hours by 2030** — more than double — driven largely by AI growth.
- It's not just electricity: cooling all those machines also consumes huge volumes of **water**, with usage projected to hit trillions of litres by 2030.

**Why it matters:** The AI tools many of us now use daily carry a real-world energy and water cost, raising pressure to power data centres with cleaner electricity and cool them more efficiently.

*Source: UN University (UNU-INWEH) report, via Business Standard / Earth.Org, June 2026*

**US and Iran Strike a Peace Deal**

**Why it matters:** Reopening the Strait of Hormuz could ease global oil prices and inflation, while the unresolved nuclear question signals the peace is fragile and far from final.

## 2026-06-14

### Topic: AI Technology

**Gemini 3.1 Ultra Stretches AI's Memory**

- Google released Gemini 3.1 Ultra, its newest flagship AI model. Its headline feature is a **2-million-token context window** — roughly the amount of text in several long novels that the model can "hold in mind" at once when answering you.
- A token is just a chunk of text (about ¾ of a word). A bigger context window means the AI can read and reason over much more material in a single conversation without forgetting earlier parts.
- It is **natively multimodal**, meaning the same model handles text, images, audio, and video together rather than needing separate tools.
- A new sandboxed *Code Execution* tool lets the model write, run, and test code mid-conversation — useful for data tasks like the Python work you do.

**Why it matters:** Larger context windows let AI tackle whole documents, codebases, or datasets in one go, reducing the manual chopping-up that limits today's tools.

*Source: Crescendo AI News / LLM-Stats, June 2026*
